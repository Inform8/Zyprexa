Pharmacology

Understanding the pharmacology of olanzapine

A broad receptor-binding profile contributes to its pharmacological effects.

Olanzapine is an antipsychotic, antimanic and mood-stabilising agent with activity across several neurotransmitter receptor systems.{{c:1:5.1}}

Its pharmacological profile includes activity at serotonin, dopamine, muscarinic, histamine and alpha-1 adrenergic receptors. The relative contribution of individual receptor interactions to clinical efficacy and adverse effects should be interpreted carefully.

Dopamine receptor activity D1, D2, D3, D4, D5

Olanzapine has affinity for dopamine receptors, including D2 receptors.{{c:1:5.1}}

Dopamine receptor antagonism is relevant to its antipsychotic activity. Dopaminergic signalling is involved in multiple brain functions, and the relationship between receptor activity and individual symptom domains is complex.

Serotonin receptor activity 5-HT2A/2C, 5-HT3, 5-HT6

Olanzapine also has affinity for serotonin receptors, including 5-HT2A and 5-HT2C receptors.{{c:1:5.1}}

Its serotonin and dopamine receptor activity contributes to its overall pharmacological profile. However, the clinical effects of olanzapine cannot be attributed to one receptor interaction alone.

Histamine H1 receptors H1

Preclinical binding profiles indicate affinity for histamine H1 receptors. In vitro affinity does not translate directly into clinical efficacy, potency, or a causal link to a particular adverse reaction.{{c:1:5.1}}

Muscarinic receptors M1–M5

Preclinical binding profiles indicate affinity for muscarinic M1–M5 receptors, which may be relevant to observed clinical tolerability. In vitro affinity does not by itself establish a clinical effect.{{c:1:5.1}}

Alpha-1 adrenergic receptors α1

Preclinical binding profiles indicate affinity for alpha-1 adrenergic receptors, which may be relevant to observed clinical tolerability. In vitro affinity does not by itself establish a clinical effect.{{c:1:5.1}}

These interactions help explain why treatment selection and monitoring must consider both therapeutic objectives and potential adverse effects.

Receptor occupancy in patients · Figure 2

5-HT2 and D2 occupancy across the approved dose range

Olanzapine demonstrated a greater in vitro affinity for serotonin 5-HT2 than dopamine D2 receptors, and greater 5-HT2 than D2 activity in in vivo models. In a single 10 mg dose PET study in healthy volunteers, it produced a higher 5-HT2A than D2 occupancy.{{c:pi:5.1}}

A PET study in patients with schizophrenia measured occupancy at steady state after random assignment to fixed daily doses.{{c:kapur}}

  • 5-HT2: greater than 90% occupancy in every patient, including at 5 mg/day.
  • D2: occupancy rose with dose, averaging 55% at 5 mg, 73% at 10 mg, 75% at 15 mg and 76% at 20 mg/day.
  • Across 10–20 mg/day: individual D2 occupancy ranged from 71% to 80%.

Limitations. Twelve patients (three per dose); striatal D2 measured with [11C]raclopride; baseline occupancy estimated from age-corrected controls rather than each patient's own scan. Receptor occupancy does not by itself predict the clinical response of an individual patient.
