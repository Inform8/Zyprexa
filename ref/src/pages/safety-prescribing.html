Dosing and administration

Recommended starting doses for oral olanzapine in adults, from the European SmPC.{{c:1:4.2}}

Table 1Recommended starting dose by treatment setting
Approved treatment settingStarting dose
Schizophrenia10 mg
once daily
Moderate to severe manic episode, monotherapy15 mg

Moderate to severe manic episode, combination therapy10 mg
once daily
Prevention of recurrence in bipolar disorder following response to olanzapine for mania10 mg

The European SmPC describes an individualised daily dose range of 5–20 mg for these treatment settings. Dose adjustments should be based on clinical assessment. An increase above the recommended starting dose should occur only after appropriate clinical reassessment and generally at intervals of not less than 24 hours.{{c:1:4.2}}

For patients who have been receiving olanzapine for a manic episode, therapy for preventing recurrence continues at the same dose. If a new manic, mixed or depressive episode occurs, olanzapine treatment should be continued (with dose optimisation as needed), with supplementary therapy to treat mood symptoms, as clinically indicated.{{c:1:4.2}}

Olanzapine can be given without regard to meals, as absorption is not affected by food.

Special populations

Older patients (65 years and over)
A lower starting dose (5 mg/day) is not routinely indicated but should be considered when clinical factors warrant.
Renal and/or hepatic impairment
A lower starting dose (5 mg) should be considered. In moderate hepatic insufficiency (cirrhosis, Child-Pugh Class A or B), the starting dose should be 5 mg and only increased with caution.
Factors that may slow metabolism
When more than one factor is present (female gender, geriatric age, non-smoking status), consider decreasing the starting dose. Dose escalation should be conservative.
Smokers
Starting dose and range need not be routinely altered. Metabolism may be induced by smoking; clinical monitoring is recommended and a dose increase may be considered if necessary.
Children and adolescents
Not recommended below 18 years of age due to a lack of data on safety and efficacy.

Source: EU SmPC section 4.2.{{c:1:4.2}}

02 · SmPC 4.3

Contraindications

The training material lists further conditions as contraindications. In the European SmPC these are warnings or precautions, covered in the next section.

Contraindicated

According to the European SmPC, oral Zyprexa is contraindicated in patients with:{{c:1:4.3}}

  • Hypersensitivity to olanzapine or to any of the excipients listed in section 6.1
  • Known risk of narrow-angle glaucoma

Excipients include lactose monohydrate. SmPC section 6.1 also lists the tablet-core and colour-coating excipients, which differ by strength. That full qualitative list is not reproduced here; consult section 6.1 of the current SmPC for the strength to be prescribed.

03 · SmPC 4.4

Important warnings and precautions

During antipsychotic treatment, improvement in the patient's clinical condition may take several days to some weeks. Patients should be closely monitored during this period.{{c:1:4.4}}

Not recommended

Dementia-related psychosis and/or behavioural disturbances

Olanzapine is not recommended for patients with dementia-related psychosis and/or behavioural disturbances because of an increase in mortality and the risk of cerebrovascular accident. In placebo-controlled trials (6–12 weeks) in elderly patients (mean age 78 years):{{c:1:4.4}}

3.5% vs 1.5%incidence of death, olanzapine vs placebo (2-fold increase)
1.3% vs 0.4%cerebrovascular adverse events, olanzapine vs placebo (3-fold increase)

The efficacy of olanzapine was not established in these trials.

Metabolic effects

Olanzapine treatment has been associated with weight gain and changes in glucose and lipid parameters. Hyperglycaemia and the development or worsening of diabetes, occasionally associated with ketoacidosis or coma, have been reported uncommonly, including some fatal cases.{{c:1:4.4}}

Assess relevant baseline risk factors and monitor weight, blood glucose and lipid parameters in accordance with the approved product information and applicable clinical guidance. See monitoring.

Somnolence and dizziness

Somnolence and dizziness may occur. Patients should be cautioned about operating machinery, including motor vehicles.{{c:1:4.7}}

Sedation and orthostatic hypotension should be considered when assessing falls risk, concomitant medication and individual tolerability.

Neuroleptic malignant syndrome

Neuroleptic malignant syndrome is a potentially life-threatening condition associated with antipsychotic medicines. Clinical manifestations include hyperpyrexia, muscle rigidity, altered mental status and autonomic instability.{{c:1:4.4}}

If a patient develops signs and symptoms indicative of NMS, or presents with unexplained high fever without additional clinical manifestations of NMS, all antipsychotic medicines, including olanzapine, must be discontinued.

Tardive dyskinesia and other movement disorders

Movement-related adverse effects may occur. The risk of tardive dyskinesia increases with long-term exposure; patients should be assessed for relevant symptoms during treatment.{{c:1:4.4}}

If signs or symptoms of tardive dyskinesia appear, dose reduction or discontinuation should be considered. These symptoms can temporarily deteriorate or even arise after discontinuation of treatment.

Cardiovascular and orthostatic effects

Orthostatic hypotension and dizziness may occur. Caution is required in patients with relevant cardiovascular risk factors or when medicines with potential interactions are co-administered. Blood pressure should be measured periodically in patients over 65 years.{{c:1:4.4}}

Hepatic and haematological considerations

Transient, asymptomatic elevations of hepatic aminotransferases (ALT, AST) have been seen commonly, especially in early treatment. Follow-up is important in patients with elevated liver enzymes, signs of hepatic impairment, limited hepatic functional reserve or concomitant use of potentially hepatotoxic medicines. If hepatitis is diagnosed, olanzapine treatment should be discontinued.{{c:1:4.4}}

Caution is also required in patients with low leukocyte or neutrophil counts, medicines known to cause neutropenia, a history of drug-induced bone marrow depression, or hypereosinophilic or myeloproliferative conditions. Neutropenia has been reported commonly when olanzapine and valproate are used concomitantly.

Other warnings in the SmPC

Summarised from SmPC section 4.4.{{c:1:4.4}} Consult the full text for each.

Parkinson's disease

Not recommended for dopamine agonist-associated psychosis in Parkinson's disease; worsening of parkinsonian symptoms and hallucinations was reported very commonly.

QT interval

Caution with medicines known to increase QTc, especially in the elderly and in congenital long QT syndrome, congestive heart failure, heart hypertrophy, hypokalaemia or hypomagnesaemia.

Thromboembolism

Venous thromboembolism has been reported uncommonly. Identify risk factors such as immobilisation and take preventive measures.

Seizures

Use cautiously in patients with a history of seizures or factors that may lower the seizure threshold.

Sudden cardiac death

Reported post-marketing. In an observational study, the risk with olanzapine was approximately twice that in patients not using antipsychotics, and comparable to pooled atypical antipsychotics.

Anticholinergic activity

Caution in prostatic hypertrophy, paralytic ileus and related conditions.

General CNS activity

Caution with alcohol and other centrally acting medicines. Olanzapine may antagonise the effects of dopamine agonists.

Lactose

Patients with rare hereditary galactose intolerance, total lactase deficiency or glucose-galactose malabsorption should not take this medicine.

Drug interactions

Olanzapine metabolism may be affected by medicines or substances that influence CYP1A2 activity. Review concomitant medicines, smoking status and relevant changes during treatment. Dose adjustment and clinical monitoring should follow the approved product information.{{c:1:4.5}}

06 · SmPC 4.6

Pregnancy and breastfeeding

Because human experience is limited, olanzapine should be used in pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the foetus.{{c:1:4.6}}

Newborns exposed to antipsychotics, including olanzapine, during the third trimester are at risk of adverse reactions including extrapyramidal and/or withdrawal symptoms, and should be monitored carefully.

Olanzapine is excreted in breast milk. Patients should be advised not to breastfeed an infant if they are taking olanzapine.

Content-document note: pregnancy and breastfeeding statements must be checked against current approved local wording.
07 · SmPC 4.2, 4.4

Discontinuation

Acute symptoms such as sweating, insomnia, tremor, anxiety, nausea or vomiting have been reported rarely when olanzapine is stopped abruptly.{{c:1:4.4}}

Gradual tapering of the dose should be considered when discontinuing olanzapine. Treatment changes should be managed by the treating healthcare professional.{{c:1:4.2}}

08 · SmPC 4.8

Adverse reactions

Frequently reported adverse reactions include somnolence, weight gain, increased appetite, dizziness, orthostatic hypotension and changes in glucose and lipid parameters. Other adverse reactions are described in the full product information, which gives the complete profile, frequency categories and qualifications.{{c:1:4.8}}

Pharmacovigilance

Reporting of suspected adverse reactions

Reporting suspected adverse reactions after authorisation of the medicinal product is important. It allows continued monitoring of the benefit/risk balance of the medicinal product. Healthcare professionals are asked to report any suspected adverse reactions via the national reporting system in Appendix V of the product information for their market.{{c:1:4.8}}
